# Required Discussion 22.1: Use Cases for Parallel Computing

Parallel computing has become a key driver of innovation across many industries. Two implementations that I find particularly impactful are artificial intelligence (AI) and utility infrastructure analytics.

The first example is AI model training. Modern AI systems such as ChatGPT, autonomous vehicle models, and image recognition systems rely heavily on GPU-based parallel computing. Training large language models involves processing billions of parameters and massive datasets simultaneously across thousands of GPUs. Without parallel computing, training these models could take years instead of weeks or months. One major challenge is the significant cost of infrastructure, power consumption, and cooling requirements. Another challenge is efficiently distributing workloads and managing communication between thousands of processors. Despite these challenges, parallel computing has enabled breakthroughs in generative AI, autonomous systems, and advanced robotics that would have been impossible using traditional sequential computing.

The second example is utility asset and infrastructure analytics. In gas utilities such as SoCalGas, large datasets containing service records, inspection data, leak surveys, GIS information, and work orders can contain millions of records. Parallel processing frameworks such as DASK, Apache Spark, and Databricks allow these datasets to be analyzed much faster than traditional methods. Challenges include maintaining data quality, handling distributed data storage, and ensuring consistent results when processes run across multiple nodes. However, parallel computing enables more advanced risk modeling, predictive maintenance, infrastructure planning, and safety analytics.

I believe both examples can lead to the development of entirely new technologies. AI is already producing capabilities that were previously considered impractical because computing power was insufficient. Similarly, utility analytics can benefit from real-time digital twins, predictive failure detection, and automated operational decision-making. Many of these innovations were previously limited by hardware constraints, memory limitations, and processing times. As parallel computing continues to evolve through GPUs, distributed clusters, and cloud platforms, the ability to process massive amounts of data quickly will continue driving innovation across industries and creating solutions that were not previously possible.
